### [PersonaFactory: Stylistic User Prompt Augmentation with Synthetic Persona Bank](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34785v1)
- Bich Ngoc Doan, Hanna Yukhymenko, Imanol Schlag
- 2026-11-25 · cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.88

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language models increasingly replace real people in the AI development pipeline, as they are used to generate training dialogues at scale and to drive alignment and evaluation. However, out-of-the-box models produce monolingual and stylistically flat texts as synthetic user prompts, failing to replicate the real user-AI communication mechanics. We present PersonaFactory, an end-to-end pipeline that generates detailed user personas and uses them to augment existing user prompts from their point of view, producing a bank of over 740000 structured synthetic personas detailing their background and communication behaviors, and a corpus of roughly 1.24 million persona-styled synthetic conversations spanning 85 languages. We evaluate the effect of personal features on style adoption and intent preservation, using an LLM-judge and heuristic metrics to assess augmentation fidelity together with the downstream impact on assistant persona control through training data. Results show that our structured, attribute-based style representation enables per-axis control and better intent preservation at comparable style fidelity than free-text persona descriptions used in existing methods. A human expert evaluation found the augmented conversations plausible, with favorable ratings for intent preservation, style naturalness, and multilingual authenticity. Finally, PersonaFactory conversations achieve comparable performance to original training data on capability benchmarks, are preferred on real user prompts at equal reply length, and adapt their reply style more closely to users without explicit steering.

### [State Space Attention: Learning Context-Dependent Memory Retention](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33625v1)
- Ege Çakar, Cengiz Pehlevan
- 2026-11-25 · cs.LG, cs.CL · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.85

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Standard attention's key-value cache grows linearly with context length. Bounding it raises the question of what a sequence model should retain for long-context reasoning. We argue this choice should be learned, and introduce State Space Attention (SSA), which learns to keep a bounded set of past representations and reads them with standard attention. A learned scorer rescores every chunk against newly arriving context and the rest of memory. A fixed-context Transformer is then the special case that always keeps the most recent tokens. We train the scorer and reader jointly, either moving gradually from a differentiable soft weighting of memories to hard retention, or with hard retention from the start. To produce signal for the scorer, we generate supervised samples during training, a technique we call recovery. We compare SSA with recurrent models, bounded-memory attention methods, and full-attention Transformers across retrieval, variable tracking, multi-hop reasoning, and language modeling. On our retrieval and reasoning benchmarks, SSA matches or comes within a few points of full attention on most tasks, far outperforms a Transformer with the same readable budget on every long-context retrieval task we evaluate, and matches or outperforms one with about 4 times that budget, while using constant inference memory. The largest remaining gap to full attention at small language model scale is on RULER. SSA makes a fixed number of tokens useful over a longer context, and since it reads memory with standard attention, we propose that it can be stacked with KV cache eviction methods. We also pretrain and post-train 400M-parameter models and measure how SSA's training and inference costs scale with context length and model size. Together, these experiments examine the capabilities and limitations of learned memory retention for long-context sequence modeling.

### [Failure Broadens, Success Sharpens: Continual Adaptation of Tool-Use Agents Across Diverse Tasks with Dual-Polarity Memory](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33612v1)
- Tianmai M. Zhang, Hoda Shajari, Amr Hendy, Xiaohu Liu, Piyush Behre et al.
- 2026-11-25 · cs.CL · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.88

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language model agents can self-improve over time by utilizing procedural memory derived from prior task trajectories, yet the distinct utilities of memories derived from local successes and failures remain underexplored. This work studies this question in a realistic setting with heterogeneous streams of multi-domain tool-use tasks and various environment constraints common in real-world scenarios. We develop \emph{Dual-Polarity Memory with Periodic Maintenance} (DualPM), a simple, retrieval-free memory workflow characterized by (1) customizable reflection and procedural memory generation from both local successes and failures within each trajectory, and (2) periodic memory consolidation over long task streams under a given memory capacity, preventing memory explosion. Online prequential evaluation with fine-grained metrics reveals that failure memory more strongly broadens coverage, success memory better streamlines execution, while combining both polarities effectively integrates both benefits. Further studies demonstrate that DualPM enables cross-domain memory generalization and continual memory adaptation, while external evaluation on EvoMemBench reveals the portability and effectiveness of DualPM as a general long-term procedural memory method.
</details>

### [TASK Memory: Beyond Text-Only Memory with Activation Steering and Knowledge Editing against Memory Hallucination](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34762v1)
- Jinkun Zhao, Wenjun Wu
- 2026-11-25 · cs.AI · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.88

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

LLM agents increasingly rely on external memory, yet they still hallucinate about what they remember. Often the correct memory is retrieved and placed in the prompt, but the answer contradicts it or the memory is applied to the wrong question. We call these use-time memory hallucinations. Across five open models, we find that they arise from the conflict between parametric knowledge and external memory and take three forms: memory-ignoring, where the model returns to a stale fact in its weights (up to 46% of queries); memory-misapplying, where it attributes the memory to unrelated entities (up to 24%, and 49% when retrieval is combined with weight editing); and version confusion, where 53% of answers about revised facts assert the superseded value when both versions remain stored. We propose TASK memory, which goes beyond text-only memory by combining a Text note with Activation Steering and Knowledge editing that share one in-model address. The text holds the content. A removable knowledge edit supplies the address through its subject key and supports multi-hop composition. A pair of steering vectors decides, query by query, whether the model adopts the memory or keeps its own answer; the vectors store no content and control how the text memory is used. On frozen models under a pre-registered, test-once protocol, TASK reduces memory-ignoring hallucinations to at most 1.5% in all ten model-dataset cells and cuts neighbour misuse from 39.2% to 7.5% (+10.8 pp composite on CounterFact). It improves a 300-memory library over BM25 RAG by 16.4 pp, an advantage that holds at 3,000 memories, resolves fact revisions without fact IDs, and adds 7.5 pp of multi-hop accuracy on MQuAKE. 21 of 23 cross-model comparisons (8B-27B, four families) are significant, and TASK beats three recent context-steering methods in 9 of 10 model-dataset cells.
</details>

### [Procedure Inference as Memory: Learning Recruiter Decision Procedures from OKF-Structured Episodes in Multi-Session Hiring Assistants](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34576v1)
- Praveen Kumar Bodigutla, Yizhi Zhang, Sai Vivek Kanaparthy, Arup De
- 2026-11-25 · cs.AI, cs.HC, cs.IR · Matched: persona + memory, episodic memory · Relevance: 0.84

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Multi-session hiring assistants must infer evolving recruiter preferences and decision patterns across projects and time. We introduce Open Knowledge Format (OKF)-indexed episodic memory to organize interactions and retrieve related events through their lineage. We also introduce Procedure Memory, which learns reusable decision procedures from these episodes through prompted reflection or reinforcement learning. We compare these approaches with no memory, full-history context, and conversation search, and evaluate Jev-class models for index navigation and GPT-5.6 Luna as a lower-cost alternative to GPT-5.4. In our evaluation, OKF-indexed retrieval achieves low-priority preference F1 scores of 0.93-0.95 at medium and high preference overlap between hiring projects. It significantly outperforms full-history context and conversation search in both settings, and no memory at high overlap, while using 69% fewer context tokens than full history. Procedure Memory reduces conversational turns by up to 39% relative to no memory. Qualitative and long-horizon evaluations provide evidence of temporal reasoning, with RL-learned procedures achieving 60% on both correct abstention and exact-field identification. Jev-class models improve conversational efficiency but more frequently miss temporally relevant evidence, highlighting the importance of evidence selection. On PersonaMem-v2, Procedure Memory with OKF retrieval achieves 50.2% accuracy versus 48.6% for full-history context and 29.0% for no memory, using 90.5% fewer reader input tokens than full history. GPT-5.6 Luna performs comparably to GPT-5.4 at lower cost. These findings highlight the value of structured episodic memory and learned decision procedures for efficient personalization and temporal reasoning.
</details>

### [AI-human hybrid conversations: Can AI Agents Improve Discourse Quality?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33459v1)
- Ishita Chakraborty, Xingjian You, Shrabastee Banerjee
- 2026-11-24 · cs.CL · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.86

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Conversations are central to how people exchange information, share experiences, and develop collective understanding. Yet, across conversational settings, discussions often suffer from uneven participation, unanswered contributions, and a narrow range of perspectives. As generative AI agents increasingly participate alongside humans, hybrid human-AI conversations present a new opportunity to address these challenges. We investigate whether AI agents can serve as catalysts for human interaction, improving conversational engagement and broadening collective discourse. We develop a framework for introducing generic and persona-conditioned AI agents into evolving discussions and evaluate it through two complementary studies. First, using agent-based simulations grounded in Reddit discussions across four domains, we find that AI-generated conversations are more positive and less toxic than human conversations, but substantially less semantically diverse. Persona conditioning produces more human-like contributions and narrows this diversity gap, while increasing the representation of underrepresented personas can shift which perspectives emerge without necessarily increasing overall diversity. Second, we conduct a pre-registered experiment with 221 participants across nine discussion threads, comparing human-only conversations with those involving generic or persona-conditioned AI agents. We find that AI participation increases responsiveness, reduces unanswered contributions, and broadens the range of ideas expressed by human participants, even though the AI agents themselves do not fully reproduce human conversational diversity. These findings suggest that the value of hybrid human-AI conversations lies not simply in the content AI agents generate, but also in their potential to stimulate more responsive and diverse interactions among humans.
</details>

### [LogToPersona: New Benchmark and Efficient Persona Prediction through Semantic Log Filtering](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33510v1)
- Ahmed Alajrami, Ondrej Bohdal, Seokwoo Song, Taegeon Um, Peyman Hosseini et al.
- 2026-11-24 · cs.CL · Matched: persona + memory · Relevance: 0.83

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Detailed knowledge of a user's persona is valuable for personalizing generative AI models and agents, yet such details are difficult to obtain and change over time. We address the task of inferring persona attributes from a single day of mobile activity logs. No suitable publicly available benchmark exists for this task, so we introduce a synthetic benchmark of 1,600 persona-log pairs. We target on-device inference so that raw logs never leave the phone, which makes memory rather than speed the limiting constraint: a day of activity yields roughly 30K tokens, whose key-value cache alone exceeds the INT4 weights of a 4B-parameter model. We therefore propose a log filtering method that retains only behaviorally distinct entries. Filtering reduces the memory footprint by up to 39% across nine models from four families and more than halves prefill time, while maintaining or improving prediction quality. For Qwen3-4B it removes 2.5GB, bringing the model from well beyond a realistic per-process budget to a manageable one. Within our benchmark, these results indicate that preserving diverse behavioral events matters more for persona prediction than retaining every semantically similar log entry.
</details>

### [Who Attributes a Mind to Whom? Mutual Theory of Mind in Simulated User-Assistant Conversation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33327v1)
- Mingxi Yan, Vasudha Varadarajan, Qiaosi Wang, Qing Xiao, Hong Shen et al.
- 2026-11-24 · cs.CL, cs.HC · Matched: persona + memory · Relevance: 0.81

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

When users assume that an AI chatbot has mental states, they are more likely to develop over-reliance, emotional dependence, and in extreme cases, psychological harm or self-harm. However, how these mind attributions arise in conversations and how human or AI factors influence this is still unknown. We study how users attribute minds using a minimal framework: a simulated user with different characteristics chats with assistants that differ in social style and memory. We also introduce a message-level measure of who attributes a mind to whom in four directions, checked with trained coders and applied by a fine-tuned encoder that reproduces an LLM judge. Our results show that, on average, nine out of thirteen characteristics raised simulated users' attribution to the assistant. We found that relational needs such as attachment anxiety raised it as theory predicts, some general personality traits raised it as well, and AI-specific characteristics the least. By contrast, the assistant's social style barely changed simulated users' mind attribution. Instead, sycophantic instructions raised the assistant's attribution to itself. Memory raised users' attribution only with some user models. The model playing the user changed overall attribution and the effects of characteristics, style, and memory. Our framework measures how persona descriptions affect mind attribution in simulation. It gives concrete hypotheses for human studies and shows the need for more consistent user simulators.
</details>
