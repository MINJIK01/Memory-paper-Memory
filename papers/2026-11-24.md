### [Evaluating Persona Consistency in LLM User Simulators Without External Reference Annotations](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30280v1)
- Anping Zhang, Yang Tan, Yipeng Liu, Clive Bai, Blaze Chen et al.
- 2026-11-23 · cs.CL, cs.AI · Matched: persona + consistency · Relevance: 0.89

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Standard evaluations assess LLM-simulated personas against an external ground truth, such as community trait annotations, but that reference is unavailable when the persona is inferred only from a short, task-oriented chat. We ask how persona consistency can be evaluated without such a reference, and propose a self-anchored internal-consistency diagnostic: the persona describes itself once and acts twice. A Likert self-report supplies a soft internal anchor; two behavioral profiles come from constrained scenario choices scored without a judge and from open-ended responses judged by an LLM. Consistency is the correlation between the self-report and each behavioral profile, in a shared six-facet BFI-2 space. The framework compares persona-construction strategies by assessing how consistently their elicited responses reflect their self-reported traits. Across ordinary-user chats (WildChat and LMSYS) and well-documented personas with community annotations (the Personality Database, PDB), profile-enriched representations agree with the anchor more closely than a raw-dialogue baseline. On PDB, this self-anchored ordering reproduces the community-annotation ordering. The pattern is stable across answering and judge models.

### [Can Cities Have Personalities? Introducing the SCD City Personality Assessment](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30733v1)
- Andrei Khurshudov
- 2026-11-23 · cs.AI, cs.CY · Matched: persona + consistency · Relevance: 0.79

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Descriptions of cities are shaped by human experience and institutional narratives. We introduce the SCD City Personality Assessment (SCD-CPA), a method that instantiates document-grounded ChatGPT city personas and interrogates each with an identical 24-statement instrument inspired by the four-pair structure of the Myers-Briggs Type Indicator. Rather than asking a general-purpose model to characterize cities freely, we configure a separate document-grounded conversational agent for each city and use alternative evidence portraits to compare documentary inputs. The resulting four bipolar dimensions and four-letter summaries use independently defined urban concepts and scoring rules. Tokyo, New York, and Paris form the methodological evaluation (33 assessments across repeated sessions, three GPT-6 effort settings, and alternative portraits): Tokyo's original portrait yielded 85.4% exact within-setting item agreement, while evidence substitutions shifted some dimension strengths. A five-city application to Shanghai, Dubai, Mumbai, Lagos, and Sao Paulo (15 assessments) yielded 77.8% exact and 99.4% within-one-point cross-setting agreement, indicating consistency under the tested configurations. By treating each city conversational agent as a reproducible, evidence-conditioned experimental configuration, SCD-CPA makes documented city-character perceptions comparable and exposes their sensitivity to source selection. Although the documentary evidence is explicitly controlled, the model's internal interpretation of that evidence cannot be fully observed. We also examine the robustness of SCD-CPA across three AI reasoning-effort settings. The observed consistency suggests that the methodology is relatively insensitive to the tested configuration differences and provides a framework for evaluating its stability as future generations of AI models become available.
</details>

### [MMMem: Self-Improving Agents with Task-Aware Multimodal Experience Consolidation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30287v1)
- Yuxuan Chen, Changwei Lv, Yunfei Li, Zhongjing Du, Kehui Tan et al.
- 2026-11-23 · cs.AI · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.87

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

AI agents accumulate conversations, images, and documents through long-term interactions with users. Answering later questions often requires connecting evidence across these records, yet the resulting understanding is rarely retained, allowing mistakes to recur on related questions. Existing multimodal memory methods largely focus on storing and retrieving records, with limited mechanisms for learning from completed tasks and consolidating task-specific experience. We therefore propose MMMem, a task-aware multimodal memory framework that extracts and consolidates experience from completed tasks through four complementary strategies. First, source-linked multi-view memory organizes original text, images, and rendered pages alongside generated scene images that combine clues across records for retrieval. Second, source-generated tasks support knowledge alignment at two levels: warm-up consolidates source knowledge into task-relevant experiences in external memory, while self-training adapts the model's parametric knowledge to task demands. Third, the same vision-language model distills completed tasks into experiences that record the task context, conclusion, and support links. Finally, task-aware retrieval recalls source views and relevant experiences for answering and verification, while source-supported reuse guides long-term retention. Across three memory benchmarks and two vision-language readers, MMMem outperforms eight baselines. With Qwen3.5-9B, MMMem achieves relative gains of 36.9%, 17.6%, and 15.2% over the best-performing baselines on LoCoMo, Mem-Gallery, and LooGLE v2, respectively. Ablations show consistent gains from training and memory warm-up, while case studies illustrate how individual components work in practice.
</details>

### [Retrieval-Augmented Generation in the Agentic Era: Tradition, Present, and Prospect](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30181v1)
- Chenming Tang, Haolin Li, Yiran Li, Zixian Yang, Lizhu Zhai et al.
- 2026-11-23 · cs.CL · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.86

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) couples frozen language models with external, updatable knowledge, so that generation is grounded in retrieved evidence rather than in parametric memory alone. This work maps RAG in the agentic era by joining them on a single lifecycle axis, from offline indexing through pre-retrieval, retrieval, post-retrieval, and orchestration over these stages. It treats indexing and orchestration as first-class components and agentic workflows as a paradigm of their own, and it covers the agentic work that has appeared since those reviews. Beyond these components, it covers evaluation, applications, and the questions that remain open for future exploration. From tradition to present and beyond, this work systematically reviews RAG from a perspective of the agentic era.

### [Composing Independently Distilled Memory and Retrieval Policies for Long-Term Agents](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30230v1)
- Grigorii Davydenko, Artem Sakhno, Igor Pavlov, Julia Belikova, Andrey Savchenko et al.
- 2026-11-23 · cs.LG · Matched: persona + memory · Relevance: 0.87

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Long-lived Web assistants require effective memory and retrieval mechanisms to access information from past user interactions. While these capabilities can be learned through specialized policies, it remains unclear whether specialists trained independently in different environments can be effectively composed without joint optimization. This paper investigates compositional transfer through policy distillation. Memory-writing and sequential retrieval policies are independently distilled from frontier-model action trajectories into 3B models and combined with a fixed answer generator without downstream training. On a LongMemEval-based benchmark, the distilled composition achieves 73.1% accuracy, compared with 36.2% for a composition of existing RL-trained specialists, and also outperforms the evaluated RL-trained compositions on PersonaMem-v2. Component ablations reveal that improvements in individual policies depend on their composition, highlighting the importance of cross-specialist compatibility. Further task-specific adaptation improves accuracy to 75.2% on LongMemEval through RL and 48.9% on PersonaMem-v2 through supervised distillation. These findings demonstrate the effectiveness of independent specialist distillation for modular multi-agent systems. Source code and datasets of teacher-generated memory-editing and retrieval trajectories are released to facilitate reproducibility and further research.
</details>

### [STMemoLLM: A Spatial-Temporal Memory-Augmented LLM for Robust Traffic Forecasting](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30148v1)
- Jian Zhou, Binqing Wu, Zongjiang Shang, Wanghui Qiu, Dongliang Cui et al.
- 2026-11-23 · cs.LG · Matched: memory-augmented · Relevance: 0.77

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Traffic forecasting is essential for intelligent transportation systems. However, traditional deep-learning models often rely heavily on high-quality observations. Recent LLM-based approaches incorporate pretrained knowledge to improve forecasting, but they remain sensitive to input perturbations and struggle to preserve long-term patterns. To address this, we propose STMemoLLM, a spatial-temporal memory-augmented LLM for robust traffic forecasting. STMemoLLM introduces memory prototypes to compress inputs into compact summary tokens while extracting reliable dynamics from residual signals, achieving a balance between stability and variability. Specifically, a memory-enhanced tokenizer encodes prototypes and sensor characteristics into attention biases to guide adaptive compression of correlated sensors into semantic summary tokens. A memory-calibrated router further evaluates contextual deviation, compression deficiency, and prototype alignment to identify reliable residual signals, which complement the summaries while suppressing noise. Extensive experiments on eight real-world datasets show that STMemoLLM achieves state-of-the-art performance. Additional experiments examine point masking, additive Gaussian noise, structured missingness, and limited training data, demonstrating strong robustness. Code is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/STMemoLLM-70BF.

### [Doc-to-LoRA Analysis: Query Conditioning, Wrong Facts, and Distractor Interference](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30825v1)
- Jesper Derehag
- 2026-11-23 · cs.LG · Matched: persona + memory · Relevance: 0.85

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Doc-to-LoRA (D2L) compiles documents into low-rank adapters, allowing language models to use their contents without repeatedly processing the original text. We investigate its suitability for internalizing user memories through factual question answering, controlled interventions, and a production MidBrain memory pipeline. Across Gemma-2-2B and Qwen3-4B, D2L loses accuracy relative to in-context learning, with additional documents introducing substantial interference. Single-document errors frequently match answers to other questions about the same passage, suggesting unreliable question-answer attribution. Layer-wise interventions show that interference accumulates across depth, with more concentrated early damage at larger bank sizes on Gemma. With eight documents, applying the full bank in the initial layers gives similar accuracy to withholding all document contributions there. Source membership is detectable in intermediate states, but trained readers do not reliably resolve the interference. On PersonaMem-v2, joint compilation and normalized adapter composition achieve accuracy close to consolidated text memory when retrieved episodes remain text, but the resulting token savings are modest. Measured throughput gains also depend on model and batch size despite substantial reductions in analytical compute. Together, these findings limit the practical case for D2L as a replacement for text-based memory and motivate investigating online-learning methods that acquire personalized behavior from interactions in which memory is used.
</details>

### [DMC-5: Dual-Model Collaboration with Cache-aware Context Compression for Long-horizon Agents](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30412v1)
- Haochen Li, Xiaobing Tu, Yinggui Wang, Jinkui Ren, Xiantao Zhang
- 2026-11-23 · cs.AI · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.85

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Long-horizon agents repeatedly feed an ever-growing interaction trajectory to a specialized agent model. Prefix caching reuses computation over repeated history, but cannot prevent context growth or remove information irrelevant to the current decision. This is especially costly when the specialized agent lacks the cached-input discounts available to general-purpose models, making trajectory compression a natural alternative. However, existing compression methods on selective context risk losing global information, recursively rewriting summary leads to semantic drift, while repeatedly recompressing the full history shifts the growing cost to the compressor. Therefore, we introduce DMC-5 (Dual-Model Collaboration with CaChe-aware Context Compression), an asymmetric framework that assigns full-history integration to a general compression model and action generation to a specialized agent model. DMC-5 maintains the raw trajectory as an Append-only Canonical View, preserving global access while allowing the compressor to reuse a stable historical prefix instead of recursively rewriting or recomputing prior context. The agent operates only on a compact Decision View with recent raw interactions, reducing repeated input while retaining the information needed for the next action. DMC-5 further incorporates YAMATO (integritY-Aware Memory Abstractions for Tool Operations) to preserve execution-critical information during lossy compression. Experiments on AppWorld, OfficeBench, and 8-objective QA show that DMC-5 reduces agent model input by 38.5-57.0% and end-to-end cost by 14.5-28.0% with 78.0-85.0% compressor cache-hit rates, while improving task performance by 1.0-5.8% over the uncompressed baseline.
</details>

### [NoeVera: Can Knowledge Guide Experiments and Experiments Refine Knowledge?](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29255v1)
- Pinaki Prasad Guha Neogi, Spyros Mastorakis, Ankur Mallick, Saravan Rajmohan, Victor Rühle
- 2026-11-22 · cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.DC, cs.IR, cs.LG · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.89

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

With the growing demand for large language model (LLM) services, optimizing LLM serving systems is becoming increasingly complex, and the bottleneck is no longer predicting how a given configuration will perform. The harder problem is deciding what to investigate: which mechanisms matter for a workload, which configurations are worth testing, and what evidence a decision still needs. In this work, we present NoeVera, an agentic Second Brain that goes beyond just one-shot retrieval by turning papers, code, benchmarks, and experimental results into a persistent, provenance-preserving knowledge base with three planes: source evidence, reusable synthesis, and agent memory. Decision-aware retrieval assembles compact evidence packs, role-specific agents reason over them and invoke a simulator (Vidur or GAUSS) whenever quantitative support is needed, and every finding is written back as a scoped evidence record, so knowledge guides experiments and experiments refine knowledge. In configuration search over four serving scenarios, NoeVera comes within 5% of the optimum in a median of 6-7 simulator calls, versus 9 for Bayesian optimization and 15 for a knowledge-free agent with the same tools; on A100 GPUs, its representative configurations deliver ~1.30x vLLM-default throughput and meet every scenario's latency objective. When reused, provenance-preserving evidence records over-generalize on only 8% of applicability traps, versus 28% for a general-purpose agent memory. On the held-out 48-task split of a 72-task benchmark, NoeVera scores 29% higher than an equally equipped agent and 36% higher than its single-call retrieval counterpart (0.80 vs. 0.62 and 0.59), and even when baselines receive NoeVera's own prompts, a statistically significant part of its evidence-evolution advantage remains.

### [From Vanishing Gradients to Recursive Self-Improvement: Evaluating the Most Influential Papers in Large Language Model Evolution (1997-2026)](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29303v1)
- Saadh Jawwadh
- 2026-11-22 · cs.CL, cs.AI · Matched: long-term memory · Relevance: 0.86

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The rapid proliferation of Large Language Model (LLM) research makes it increasingly difficult to distinguish incremental refinements from true paradigm shifts. This review evaluates 22 of the most influential publications spanning nearly three decades (1997-2026), organized through a causal, chronologically anchored taxonomy centered on the author's birth year (1999). Beginning "before 1999" with how recurrent neural networks (RNNs) forgot earlier context (much like forgetting Chapter 1 by the time a reader reaches Chapter 9), we trace how Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks introduced a "bouncer" mechanism of forget, input, and output gates to selectively preserve long-term memory. We then examine how Word2Vec turned language into vector mathematics (king - male + female ~ queen), why sequential RNNs hit a parallelization wall, and how the Transformer's all-to-all attention weights launched the modern LLM era alongside a quadratic bottleneck. Next, we analyze why scaling parameters alone leaves capacity under-trained unless data scales equally (Chinchilla), how Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) left alignment superficial, and how that gap compounded into adversarial jailbreaks and single-direction refusal subspaces (J-space), where instructing a model not to think of a restricted concept surfaces it much like the human "red ball" paradox. Finally, we synthesize the current state of AI (2024-2026), encompassing recursive model collapse, autonomous reasoning-model jailbreak agents, machine-speed agentic intrusions, and closed-loop recursive self-improvement (AIDE^2).

### [ContextFact: Remembering Facts in Context for Long-Term Conversational Agents](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29155v1)
- Jiayu Huang, Yichao Cai, Xinyu Li, Zhen Zhang, Javen Qinfeng Shi
- 2026-11-22 · cs.CL, cs.AI · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.88

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Agents built on large language models must maintain continuity across repeated interactions with users. However, long-term conversations scatter facts across turns and sessions, making it difficult to retrieve the right evidence with enough context. Memory for such conversations must therefore preserve facts with their context while keeping evidence selectively accessible. We first study how memory construction and retrieval-unit boundaries affect answerability and evidence access. Reading the entire memory bank reveals gaps that cannot be resolved by retrieval selection alone, showing that some context must be preserved during memory construction. Adjacent-turn grouping improves evidence access at matched unique-turn budgets, while wider windows do not consistently help. This motivates keeping fact-specific context local while organizing cross-session evidence separately. We propose ContextFact, which links context-preserving fact memories with source-grounded entity representations. Within sessions, it gathers the context needed to interpret each fact. Across sessions, it aligns recurring referents and revisits the source dialogue to reconstruct coherent entity representations linked to the supporting facts. At query time, ContextFact retrieves fact and entity views, reranks them independently, and uses entity-linked expansion to surface related facts; complete entity representations then provide organized cross-session evidence. With Qwen3.5-27B as the backbone, ContextFact achieves state-of-the-art performance while using only $21\%$ of the evidence tokens consumed by the strongest evaluated baseline, obtaining $86.49\%$ overall judge accuracy on LoCoMo and $91.00\%$ on LongMemEval-S. Controlled ablations support context-preserving fact boundaries and source-grounded entity reconstruction, while entity readout improves multi-hop accuracy beyond adding more facts.
</details>

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Effective long-horizon persuasion in recurring goal-oriented settings requires more than factual memory: it must track how a user's trust, negotiation behavior, and strategic receptiveness evolve across interactions. Existing persuasive dialogue systems are largely single-session, static, and unimodal, while cross-session memory systems, though temporally aware, retain only factual content and overlook persuasion-specific dynamics. ### [Internalized Experience: How Training Data Shapes Memory Formation in LLMs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28613v1)
- Xianxuan Long, Xinnan Dai, Kai Guo, Jiliang Tang
- 2026-11-21 · cs.AI · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.86

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Parametric memory enables large language models (LLMs) to retain information from past experience and reuse it in subsequent tasks. Yet how training data supports the internalization of reusable task rules remains unclear. We introduce FSMem (Finite-State Machine Memory), a state-transition framework for constructing training data and studying how data content and organization shape memory formation. We evaluate rule reuse across synthetic worlds, classical planning, and code execution through valid-action identification and execution-outcome prediction, complemented by next-operation prediction in code. Our experiments reveal several systematic learning patterns. Under matched training conditions, even unchanged task rules become harder to internalize when learned alongside a larger variety of action types; by contrast, scaling the training data to provide broader state-action coverage generally improves rule learning. We further find that training at a single execution depth produces depth-specialized behavior, with models performing best when training and test executions have similar lengths. Motivated by this finding, we construct cumulative corpora that combine short and long trajectories, which can outperform long-trajectory-only training while using fewer training tokens. Together, these results demonstrate that the same task rules can be internalized differently depending on how execution experience is constructed, and that understanding these learning patterns can guide the design of more effective training data for parametric memory.
</details>

### [HyperHarness: Learning to Generate Personalized Agent Harnesses from User Histories](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29062v1)
- Zhaoxuan Tan, Jiachen Zhao, Jingbo Yang, Zheyuan Liu, Chuhan Wang et al.
- 2026-11-21 · cs.CL · Matched: persona + memory · Relevance: 0.86

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Personalized LLM agents rely on an agent harness, the surrounding memory and control code, to connect user history with downstream decisions. Existing methods typically employ either a single shared memory workflow across all users, which is efficient to deploy but cannot adapt how evidence is retrieved and interpreted across diverse histories, or per-user harness optimization, which customizes behavior for each user but requires costly repeated candidate evaluation at deployment and suffers from sparse, noisy supervision in raw logs. To address this trade-off, we present HyperHarness, a framework that learns a shared history-to-harness generator to synthesize executable, user-specific retrieval and answer-guidance code directly from user histories. Given a user's permitted history, HyperHarness prompts a construction LLM to write personalized retrieval and guidance functions, while downstream task scores across training users supervise iterative refinements to the shared generator without reference harnesses or answering-model updates. At deployment, the frozen generator constructs a harness for each unseen user in a single pass from history alone, eliminating per-user task-scored search. Across three benchmarks (PersonaMemv3, Mem-PAL, and MemoryCD) and three answering models (Qwen3.5-9B, Qwen3.8-27B, and GPT-5.6-Luna), HyperHarness achieves the highest aggregate score in seven of nine settings and ranks second in the remaining two, improving over the strongest baselines by up to 5.95 points while cutting matched answer-time tokens by 90.32% over long-context prompting. Analyses show that optimization tailors context allocation to each executor, transfers to unseen user clusters, and amortizes adaptation costs.
</details>

### [EvoViM: Recursively Self-Evolving Visual Agents for Exploration with Deferred Queries](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28574v1)
- Xinyu Cao, Tianjie Ju, Zhuosheng Zhang
- 2026-11-21 · cs.AI · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.86

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Multimodal large language model (MLLM) agents have shown promising capabilities in task-oriented environments. However, they still struggle when queries are deferred until after exploration, requiring them to retain visual evidence before knowing which details will matter. We introduce EvoViM, a recursively self-evolving framework in which a frozen multimodal model turns its own interaction experience into one visual program for memory and skills. The program retains images and computes directly on pixels, and its operations decide how this evidence is used in each task. During memory evolution, the same model traces failures in its visual trajectories to the operations that lost or misused evidence and revises them. It also rewrites the notes and analysis tools that guide later revisions, which then build on earlier findings and skip changes that have already failed. We evaluate EvoViM with three multimodal models on six tasks covering visual matching, spatial recall, and sequential decision-making, including three tasks with deferred queries. Across the six tasks, EvoViM improves the average score over AutoMem, the strongest baseline in the main comparison, by 31.3-47.6 points on held-out episodes. Programs written once from the initial environment instructions perform substantially worse, highlighting the value of interaction experience in shaping how visual evidence is retained and used. Code and dataset are available at https://evovim.github.io/.
</details>
