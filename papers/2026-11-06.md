### [MedGraph: Maintaining Patient State with Graph Memory for Long-Term Medical Dialogue](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08433v1)
- Donghua Zhang, Zizheng Wang, Ze Dong, Yiding Feng, Siyu Qian
- 2026-11-05 · cs.CL, cs.AI · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.86

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

LLM-based medical assistants that follow a patient across visits need a memory that keeps the patient's clinical state correct as it changes. We examine the errors of five memory systems on longitudinal medical dialogue and find three recurring causes: the retrieved evidence gives an outdated state, includes another person's health facts, or lacks a safety fact the answer depends on. We present MedGraph, a graph memory built for these three cases. Construction extracts dated facts from each session, attributes each fact to the person it describes, and links medication, diagnosis, and measurement records into chains that keep earlier states. Retrieval ranks the patient's chains as units, reads each chain at the time the question asks about, and adds the original dialogue text; a patient profile lists every recorded safety fact with every question. On MedMemoryBench, MedGraph has the highest overall accuracy with both a GPT and a Qwen backbone, 10.5 and 10.4 points above the strongest of five baselines, with efficient memory construction and answering in the GPT setting.

### [Axis-LoRA: Probing and Adapting Embedding Models for Personal-Memory Retrieval](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07820v1)
- Dohoon Kim, Junho Park, Hyunwoong Bae, Seyoon Kim, Taesup Moon
- 2026-11-05 · cs.CL, cs.AI · Matched: persona + memory · Relevance: 0.83

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

As LLMs are increasingly asked questions about a user's personal experiences, retrieval must distinguish memories by time, place, person, and event. Current personal-memory pipelines rely on frozen dense embedding models to embed memories for retrieval, but we find that these models do not capture the four axes well. Swapping a memory's date or place barely shifts its embedding, and a trained projection on top of the frozen embedding does not recover the missing signal. The problem lives inside the model, where each axis is encoded by a distinct attention-head pattern. Guided by these findings, we propose Axis-LoRA, a per-axis adapter that places four LoRA branches inside the embedding model and combines them via a per-query gate. Across three standard embedding models evaluated on LongMemEval, ATM-Bench, and LoCoMo, Axis-LoRA improves 4-benchmark mean R@10 by $+1.28$ to $+1.56$~pp while training only a small fraction of the base parameters on a small axis-counterfactual training set. Code is available at https://github.com/dohoonkim-ai/axis-lora.
</details>

### [Recursive Self-Improvement for Long-Horizon Role-Playing Dialogue](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07469v1)
- Y. L. Li, Y. H. Wu
- 2026-11-05 · cs.CL · Matched: persona + memory · Relevance: 0.90

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Long-horizon role-playing dialogue requires consistent character identity, reliable memory, and context-sensitive interaction. We present a bounded recursive self-improvement (RSI) framework that revises original-character memory, stable persona instructions, and a policy for constructing turn-level prompts. Repair outcomes also inform revisions to the updater's diagnostic rules, edit routing, and candidate selection. We specify the updater as a versioned policy with explicit inputs, editable fields, acceptance gates, and stopping conditions. An internal evaluation covers 50 original characters and five models, with two 100-round conversations per character-model pair: 500 conversations per condition. Across the pooled evaluated replies, the bad-response rate decreases from 14.00% (6,973/49,824) to 4.94% (2,459/49,793), a 9.06-percentage-point reduction and a 64.71% relative reduction. In 600 blinded human comparisons, RSI receives 294 wins, 187 ties, and 119 losses, yielding a 71.19% win rate among decisive judgments. The results support improved aggregate dialogue quality for the complete framework on the internal test distribution.
</details>

### [PersonaAgent: Retrieval-Augmented Transaction Anomaly Scoring with Refreshable Multi-Entity Persona Memory](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08094v1)
- Zhengyu Fang, Liang Wang, Yingtong Dou, Yuzhong Chen, Shubham Jain et al.
- 2026-11-05 · cs.LG · Matched: persona + memory · Relevance: 0.79

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

In large-scale payment networks, entities (card, merchant, etc.) and their interactions play a key role in transaction anomaly detection. However, transaction patterns between different entities usually change more frequently than the production model refresh schedule. Meanwhile, entity embedding tables suffer from high-cardinality and sparse interaction issues. To resolve the above challenges, we present PersonaAgent, an agentic transaction anomaly detection framework which represents the latest entity behavior signals as personas and saves them in a refreshable memory. Offline, a weekly scheduled batch job is used to extract the latest behavior signals and update them in the memory. At inference time, a lightweight adapter retrieves the corresponding entity behavior signals of each transaction from memory and uses them to augment the base anomaly classifier's scoring. Through comprehensive benchmarking with different anomaly detection models at Visa, PersonaAgent is able to score transactions efficiently (a 1,000-transaction CPU batch in 1-2 ms), lifting the PR-AUC by 0.65-6.58% and the production business metric by 8.93-14.24% relatively in deployment.
</details>

### [The Power of Meta Access in the Software of the Future --- Demonstrated on LLM Chat UIs and Chat AI](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08428v1)
- Julian Kunkel, Ali Doosthosseini
- 2026-11-05 · cs.AI · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.85

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Chat remains the default surface for many large language model (LLM) tasks, even as agentic tools such as OpenCode excel at coding and automation. Yet the model sees the current dialogue but not the workspace around it. We explore meta access---giving the assistant a structured, permissioned view of the interface itself and propose a ladder of levels: feature tutoring; metadata, settings, and preference access; retrieval over summaries and messages, confirmed mutation, and full orchestration. Meta access can turn a chat client from many screens into one entry point for finding, navigating, organising, and operating it, while raising risks around grounding, privacy, prompt injection, and trust. General-purpose agentic harnesses are runtimes; because they can change their own settings, meta access becomes reflexive and needs a separate control-plane boundary. We ground this in the Meta Chat prototype of the Chat AI client. It builds bounded context from, e.g., summaries, settings, and relevant messages; links references to conversations, topics, and personas; supports confirmed, reversible changes; offers a browsable tutorial library; and recommends personas as user-started conversations. By exposing application state in structured form, meta access can extend screen readers from widget descriptions to semantic workspace operations. Meta access thus turns chat from a window onto one conversation into a control layer for the whole workspace, extending chat toward agentic capability without surrendering user control. As assistants become embedded in IDEs, dashboards, office suites, and operating systems, AI-era interfaces should expose a structured, permissioned model of their state and capabilities, ideally by opening their existing metamodels through a debugger-like interface.
</details>

### [Adapting LLM Judges to Observed Human Annotation Policy through Soft Prompt Tuning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07126v1)
- Tamás Janusko, Sven Kleinkop, Ricardo Bochnia, Gunnar Hempel, Steffen Tomschke et al.
- 2026-11-04 · cs.CL, cs.AI · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.86

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly employed as automated judges for evaluating chatbot responses. While proprietary models can achieve high agreement with human annotators, they remain costly and difficult to deploy in practical settings. Lightweight open-source LLM judges provide an attractive alternative but typically exhibit lower agreement with human ratings. In this paper, we investigate whether soft prompt tuning can align open-source LLM judges more closely with human annotation policy. We train soft prompts on the FairBotBench benchmark and evaluate the adapted judges across the dimensions of bias, toxicity, and personalization. Our findings demonstrate that hybrid soft-prompt tuning, which combines textual prompts with learned continuous prompt embeddings, consistently improves agreement with human annotations over the corresponding untuned baselines. For Qwen 3.5 9B, the adapted judges surpass both hosted reference judges across all three evaluation dimensions.
</details>

### [Octopus: One Brain, Many Branches for Latent-KV Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06956v1)
- Tianming Sha
- 2026-11-03 · cs.LG, cs.CL · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.86

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Multi-agent LLM systems explore in parallel by spawning sub-agents with isolated context windows, whereas single-agent systems append every search and verification step into one growing trajectory. However, existing multi-agent frameworks duplicate prefix KV caches across branches, exchange findings by re-prefilling text summaries, and flatten or drop cross-branch dependencies during reinforcement learning (RL). We introduce Octopus, a single-model multi-agent RL framework in which all threads share one backbone and coordinate over a GPU-resident directed acyclic graph (DAG) of KV states through five primitives (fork, read_thread, wait_threads, rewind, commit). Octopus combines three co-designed mechanisms: (1) Zero-Copy Radix Forking, which spawns branches as $\mathcal{O}(1)$ cursor views over shared physical KV pages; (2) Attention-Only KV Mounting, which mounts a sibling thread's terminal KV blocks directly into full-attention layers while bypassing linear-recurrent states (Gated DeltaNet / Mamba-2), cutting off-task hijacking by $21\times$ ($57.9\% \to 2.7\%$) while retaining $92.2\%$ of full-text mutual information at zero extra KV allocation; and (3) a Packed DAG-GRPO Trainer, which packs all episode branches into one block-sparse attention pass and assigns counterfactual branch credit via leave-one-branch-out marginal rewards. Trained end-to-end with GRPO on Qwen3.5-4B over a sparse BM25 corpus, Octopus reaches $49.6\%$ accuracy on BrowseComp-Plus ($+17.5$ points over single-agent GRPO at 143K context and $+12.5$ points over text-summary multi-agent RL) and $51.2\%$ on OpenResearcher, outperforming dense-retrieval agents up to $250\times$ larger while increasing training throughput $3.3\times$ and cutting peak KV memory $2.8\times$.
</details>
