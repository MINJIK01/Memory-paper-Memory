### [PersonaChatBench: Evaluating Chat Assistants with Diverse Simulated Users](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42060v1)
- Zheyuan Deng, Taoran Li, Jingwen Zhang, Yuante Li, Dachuan Zhao et al.
- 2026-10-30 · cs.CL · Matched: persona + consistency · Relevance: 0.83

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Conversational assistants are expected to help people with diverse backgrounds, preferences, and communication styles. Yet evaluations centered on a single cooperative user provide limited insight into how reliably an assistant serves users with different personas. We introduce PersonaChatBench, a framework for evaluating task competence and responsiveness to diverse user needs. PersonaChatBench pairs the same underlying task with multiple simulated users, each guided by a private persona card that specifies distinct needs, preferences, and interaction behaviors. By holding the task fixed while varying the user, the framework examines how assistant performance changes across user conditions. We evaluate 20 language models across six domains, separately assessing native task outcomes, fulfillment of expressed persona needs, and simulator fidelity. Our results show that task performance and need fulfillment vary across personas and domains. Strong task performance does not necessarily translate into satisfying individual needs, and models' relative strengths in meeting those needs are not consistent across domains. PersonaChatBench makes these differences measurable, revealing gaps obscured by aggregate scores and helping identify which users an assistant serves well and where adaptation is needed. It provides a basis for evaluating consistency across users and guiding the development of assistants that better accommodate diverse needs in everyday applications.

### [ORION: An Outcome-Driven LLM Advisor for RL-Based Multi-Domain 6G Slice Placement](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41563v1)
- Mahmoud S. Mayaleh, Fetia Bannour, Nour-El-Houda Yellas
- 2026-10-30 · cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.MA, cs.NI · Matched: episodic memory · Relevance: 0.79

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Deploying 6G network slices across administrative domains requires an online decision for each arriving request: which domain hosts each virtual network function, how traffic crosses domain boundaries, and whether the slice is admitted. This joint decision problem is NP-hard under continuous arrivals. Experiments on a parametrized family of multi-domain topologies show that no fixed planning strategy dominates across all regimes: the performance ranking reverses between topology classes, and feasible placements exist for most rejected requests but remain undiscovered. These findings identify the plan layer as the decisive performance lever. We introduce ORION, a hierarchical orchestrator in which two LLM agents translate service intents into structured requests and generate abstract placement plans. A cooperative multi-agent RL coordinator selects the cross-domain partition, treating the plan as a persistent soft prior, while per-domain agents place the functions. ORION main contribution lies at the plan layer: an outcome-driven episodic memory that records, for each completed attempt, the network-condition profile, the proposed plan, and its verified feasibility outcome. The memory feeds verified successes and labelled failures back to the planner, thereby closing the adaptation loop using deployment feasibility rather than a scalar reward assigned to the planner's own proposals. To isolate the effect of the plan quality, we evaluate the planner on held-out topology families while keeping the downstream selection and execution mechanisms fixed. Under this controlled setting, the proposed memory improves admission by $4.6$--$11.5$ percentage points over the same planner without memory, while importance-based retention accounts for most of the improvement over a first-in-first-out store.

### [Training language models in simulation for psychiatric intake](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40476v1)
- Samuel Schmidgall, Tim Strother, Alex Bijamov, Min Woo Sun, Anil Palepu et al.
- 2026-10-29 · cs.AI, cs.CL · Matched: role play · Relevance: 0.80

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

In psychiatric practice, the clinical intake interview remains a primary diagnostic instrument, requiring the clinician to establish rapport while disentangling complex, overlapping presentations. Here, we show that multi-turn reinforcement learning (RL) in simulation environments improves language model performance on structured text-based psychiatric intake, spanning history-taking, differential diagnosis, and initial management planning. In a randomized, double-blind crossover study ($N = 60$ matched scenario pairs, 120 encounters) comparing the RL-trained agent against its identically prompted base model, 32 board-certified psychiatrists role-played standardized patients while a separate panel of 15 independent, board-certified psychiatrists scored the de-identified transcripts. To systematically probe conversational safety, we established two Delphi-consensus taxonomies, of clinical safety risks and relational competencies, from which 256 challenging adversarial presentations were derived; we find that training in simulation environments more than halved under-triage of acute safety risks (12.9% vs. 30.5%, $p < 0.0001$). Simulation environments thus provide a controlled testbed for training and stress-testing psychiatric conversational agents, with gains that transferred to psychiatrist-enacted encounters, though residual gaps require safeguards and clinician oversight.

### [Adaptive Personalization Under Preference Epistemic Uncertainty](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40930v1)
- Priyanka Dey, Rajarshi Das, Vikas Bahirwani, Qi Zeng, Julia Kruk et al.
- 2026-10-29 · cs.CL · Matched: persona + memory · Relevance: 0.83

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Human preferences change over time: an established preference may remain valid over time, become uncertain as context shifts, or be explicitly revised. Effective personalization requires conversational AI to adapt its response strategy to evolving preferences rather than relying on passive memory retrieval. We formalize this capability as adaptive personalization under epistemic uncertainty. A human preference study further confirms that people favor state-contingent response strategies as preference uncertainty changes. To evaluate whether language models mirror this adaptation, we create a multilingual benchmark spanning 250 personas, 5 languages, and 25K conversational turns that decouples strategy selection from response execution. Across eight closed- and open-weight models, we uncover a recognition-generation gap: models reliably identify appropriate strategies when selecting among candidate options, but collapse during open-ended generation, suffering a 35-46% drop in strategy calibration. Crucially, this failure is systematic: models default to exploit-first behavior, over-relying on existing preferences regardless of preference epistemic uncertainty. We show this failure is addressable: while prompting interventions fail, targeted training that supervises models' internal strategy representations toward state-conditional human preferences narrows the user acceptability generalization gap by a third on out-of-distribution personas and conversational styles.

### [Can LLMs Ignore What They Know? Measuring Knowledge Leakage in Persona-Based User Simulation](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39939v1)
- Sangmin Lee, Sangwon Lee, Sungbeom Choi, Jeeseung Park, Kibeom Myoung
- 2026-10-29 · cs.CL · Matched: semantic · Relevance: 0.86

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

When persona-conditioned responses are read as respondent-level simulations, each response should reflect the described persona's knowledge, and the respondent written to know nothing must act as if the model's knowledge were not there. We test whether it can, and find persistent favourable-item real-twin rating separation under the tested low-knowledge and explicit-ignorance prompts. We define the resulting problem (knowledge leakage: a response bias that arises when knowledge the real user could not have enters the simulated response) and present a methodology for measuring it quantitatively. The core instrument pairs real items with fabricated twins matched in surface form: screened fabricated variants designed to minimize prior item-specific knowledge. The paired difference therefore provides a diagnostic contrast for knowledge leakage even in rating tasks where no answer is correct. In a rating experiment across three knowledge-handling arms and five API endpoints from two model families, we find, first, that the personas we test do not fully suppress real-item existence and familiarity signals: a favourable-item real-twin rating contrast remains under an explicit in-context statement that the persona has never heard of the item. Second, the unfavourable-item contrast reverses sign across domain groups: pooling all unfavourable-content items across domains the contrast sits near zero (+0.077 [-0.057, +0.204]), yet the same estimator in the same conditions returns -0.359 where the rating scale asks about an item's documented hazard and +0.438 where it asks about a company or an organisation. A pooled analysis of unfavourable-content items against zero therefore does not detect the opposing domain-group differences. All estimates are conditional on the adequacy of twin construction and screening; human low-knowledge baselines remain necessary for population-level interpretation.
